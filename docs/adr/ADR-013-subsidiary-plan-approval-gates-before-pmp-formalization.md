# ADR 013: Subsidiary Plan Approval Gates Before PMP Formalization

## 1. Executive Brief
ADR-008 introduced the PMBOK 8 orchestrator, which drafts subsidiary management plans and synthesizes the integrated Project Management Plan (PMP) in one run, with a single full-authority gate, `approve-pmp`, evaluating the integrated result. The intended enterprise process is different: the PMBOK tooling **drafts** subsidiary plans, plans designated for standalone execution are **approved individually**, and only after all active route requirements are satisfied does **PMP formalization** begin.

Checked directly against the current implementation, that per-plan approval step does not exist. `scripts/orchestrate_pmbok.py --mode full` writes nine plans and immediately synthesizes `Project-Management-Plan.md`, even if a plan failed to generate; each generated plan names `approve-pmp` as its governing policy, so no plan is ever judged individually. The synthesized baseline label `PMP-BL-v1.0.0` consequently sits over subsidiary plans that were generated by the stub LLM client and never individually approved. The integrated `approve-pmp` gate itself has not been built or run either (see §3).

**The gap this closes is attribution and credit assignment.** When only the integrated PMP can be accepted or declined, a rejection or revision request does not say which subsidiary plan caused it, forcing a manual diagnostic search across all plans. Gates at each plan make every decision point at a specific plan and a specific criterion, turning each plan into its own recorded body of evidence (decision, criteria support, revision history) that later analysis and learning can draw on.

This ADR inserts a route-aware per-plan approval gate between drafting and formalization: one new full-authority `TaskPolicy`, `approve-subsidiary-plan`, evaluated per `full_plan`; approval evidence kept as ordinary `Episode` records bound to the plan's canonical content hash; and a formalization step that runs only when all active route preconditions under **ADR-011** (Project Tailoring and Route Decision) are satisfied. It extends ADR-008, harmonizes with ADR-011, and replaces the monolithic single-step semantics of ADR-008 §4.2's `--mode full`.

## 3. Context & Current Deficiencies
- **The intended sequence has three steps, the implementation has two.** Intended: draft plans $\to$ approve individual standalone plans $\to$ formalize the PMP. Implemented: draft all plans and synthesize the PMP in one atomic run $\to$ evaluate the PMP with `approve-pmp`. ADR-008 §4.1 lists "Subsidiary Management Plans & Project Management Plan" as a single step, and §4.3 defines only `approve-pmp` and `approve-adaptation-plan` as gates.
- **Plans are governed by `approve-pmp` today.** Each generated plan's frontmatter sets `governance_policy: "approve-pmp"` and its body states it is "verified under `approve-pmp`" (`scripts/orchestrate_pmbok.py`, plan template inside `run_orchestration`). The PMP gate is the only gate any plan ever meets.
- **`approve-pmp` is designed but not built (confirmed in repository).** Only an action shape (`docs/policy-shapes/approve-pmp.actions.json`) and an example decision frame (`docs/policy-shapes/decision-frames/pmp-approval.json`) exist. The local policy store holds no `approve-pmp` policy, and `docs/policy-shapes/episodes/` contains no PMP decision. Consequently `PMP-BL-v1.0.0` has never been approved; it is the label the synthesizer writes. This leaves ADR-008's `approve-pmp` gate (Story 8.1 $AC_2$) unmet.
- **A PMP-level decision cannot be attributed to a plan (The MIMO Credit Assignment Deficit).** `approve-pmp` returns one decision over the integrated document. A `reject_pmp` or `request_revision_before_decision` result names no subsidiary plan, so the maintainer must search through the plans to rectify it. The decision's per-criterion support (`OptionAssessment.criteria`, ADR-005 §4.5) attributes the problem to an integrated criterion (such as plan completeness), not to a plan.
- **A failed plan does not block synthesis.** A per-process exception is caught, printed as `[WARN] Execution error on <pid>`, and the loop continues; the PMP is then synthesized from whichever plans succeeded.
- **The stub fallback is silent.** The client is the stub whenever `--mock` is set or API keys are absent. In the committed baseline, eight of the nine plans contain `"description": "Stub content for Artifact"`, and the Quality plan carries a malformed title and generic text.
- **`--mode adapt` has the same shape.** It regenerates the plans checked in `Adaptation-Plan.md` and re-synthesizes the PMP with no plan-level approval in between.
- **Constraints already decided elsewhere:**
  - ADR-001: one `TaskPolicy` per `(agent_id, task_id)` with one stable action taxonomy; `full` authority reasons per instance over a `DecisionFrame` with hard constraints.
  - ADR-002 Invariant #2: five record types only, so approval evidence must be `Episode` records.
  - ADR-008 Invariant #4: `approve-pmp` and `approve-adaptation-plan` permanently require human approval.
  - ADR-009 §4.13: PMBOK's local `StateStore` may hold runtime lifecycle state but does not extend `LearningStore`.
  - ADR-011: Tailoring establishes that plans have routes (`full_plan`, `pmp_section`, `not_applicable`). A first cycle of "PMP only" is valid.

## 4. Technical Decision

### 4.1 Three-step route-aware sequence: Draft, Approve, Formalize
Planning is split into three separately invoked, route-aware steps:
1. **Draft:** The orchestrator generates plans according to their assigned route under ADR-011:
   - Plans routed as `full_plan` are drafted into standalone files (`docs/project-docs/management-plans/<Name>.md`).
   - Plans routed as `pmp_section` are drafted as staged files under `docs/project-docs/.staging/pmp-sections/<Name>.md`, version-controlled and canonically hashed exactly as `full_plan` files. They are consumed by the Formalize step and are not separately approved.
   - Plans routed as `not_applicable` are skipped, with their omission rationale recorded in the tailoring matrix.
   Drafting follows causal topological sequencing (System 2 DAG): Core Baselines (Scope 5.1 $\to$ Schedule 6.1 $\to$ Cost 7.1) are drafted before Operational Support plans (Quality 8.1, Resource 9.1, Communications 10.1) and Risk/Procurement/Stakeholder (11.1, 12.1, 13.2).
2. **Approve:** Each plan routed as `full_plan` is evaluated and adjudicated through `approve-subsidiary-plan` (§4.2).
3. **Formalize:** A separate orchestrator step synthesizes `Project-Management-Plan.md` and runs **only when all active route preconditions are satisfied** (§4.3). The synthesized PMP is then evaluated by the integrated `approve-pmp` gate.

### 4.2 One policy, `approve-subsidiary-plan`, evaluated per standalone plan
- **Identity:** `agent_id = agent-learning-governance`, `task_id = approve-subsidiary-plan`. One policy parameterized by `plan_id` in the `DecisionFrame` and resulting episode (ADR-001 §4.1).
- **Authority:** `full` (`DecisionResolver`), with `requires_human_approval: true` permanently, consistent with ADR-008 Invariant #4. An agent recommends and quantifies; a human adjudicates every standalone plan.
- **Actions (Standard Three-Action Taxonomy):** `approve_plan`, `reject_plan`, `request_revision_before_decision`.
- **Criteria (Weights illustrative, to be fixed at FDD level):** charter alignment (0.30); substantive content and specificity (0.30); internal completeness against PMBOK process outputs (0.25); consistency with dependent plans (0.15).
- **Technical Integrity Hard Constraints (Ruled Out Outright):** A candidate plan is ruled out from `approve_plan` outright if:
  1. *Metadata Marker:* Frontmatter contains `generator: stub` or `generator: mock`.
  2. *Placeholder Tokens:* Content contains unexpanded generator or template tokens (`Stub content for Artifact`, `[Insert here]`, `[TBD]`, `[TODO]`).
  3. *Process Failure:* Caught generation exception or missing artifact file.
- **Governance Flags (ADR-011 §3.2: Floors/Caps and Informational Tokens are Flags, Not Blocks):**
  - If a plan's total word count falls below its declared floor or exceeds its cap under ADR-011, or if it contains provisional project tokens (e.g. unbracketed `TBD` or `TODO` for tentative schedule dates or open risks), it is **flagged with evidence** in the `DecisionFrame`.
  - Floor, cap, or provisional token deviations do *not* rule out `approve_plan` mechanically; the human adjudicator decides whether the content is acceptable. If approved despite a flag, a non-empty human rationale must be recorded in the resulting `Episode.metadata`.
- **Batch Adjudication Protocol & Accountability:** To prevent human channel capacity overload across multiple gates, the CLI supports batch review:
  ```powershell
  python scripts/orchestrate_pmbok.py --mode approve-all-plans --interactive
  ```
  The interface displays an integrated dashboard of candidate plans, diffs, flags, and agent scores. While the human review experience is consolidated for ergonomic convenience, **governance accountability remains strictly decoupled and per-plan**:
  - The atomic sign-off unrolls into discrete, isolated, cryptographically hashed `Episode` records in `LearningStore` per plan.
  - **Batch sign-off requires a separate recorded non-empty rationale for every flagged plan in the batch**, ensuring the batch workflow cannot obscure individual accountability or bypass the rationale requirement.

### 4.3 Route-Aware Formalization Precondition & Canonical Hashing
The formalization step permits PMP synthesis if and only if every subsidiary plan satisfies its route-conditioned contract:
1. **For `full_plan`:** A valid `approve_plan` episode exists whose recorded hash matches the **canonical content hash** of the plan file on disk now, **AND the approval holds no active `STALE_DEPENDENCY` status** (all upstream ancestors remain valid and unchanged).
2. **For `pmp_section`:** Staged section content under `docs/project-docs/.staging/pmp-sections/<Name>.md` is present and non-stub, with any floor/cap flags surfaced for human evaluation during `approve-pmp`.
3. **For `not_applicable`:** An approved tailoring route episode exists recording a non-empty omission rationale (ADR-011 Invariant 2).

#### Canonical Content Normalization & Hash Scope Rule:
To prevent cross-platform CRLF/LF line ending mismatches, Unicode variance, or metadata changes from breaking valid approvals:
1. **Scope:** The canonical hash covers the Markdown body plus static semantic frontmatter, strictly excluding volatile metadata fields (`last_modified`, `generated_at`, `governance_hash`, `approval_ref`).
2. **Unicode & Line Normalization:** Content is NFC-normalized (`unicodedata.normalize('NFC', text)`), UTF-8 BOM is stripped (`.lstrip('\ufeff')`), and all line endings are normalized to LF (`\n`).
3. **Whitespace Discipline:** Leading and trailing outer blank lines are stripped. Trailing whitespace per line is stripped, with the explicit exception of intentional double spaces (`  \n`) representing Markdown hard line breaks.

```python
import unicodedata
import hashlib

def compute_canonical_plan_hash(content: str) -> str:
    # 1. Strip UTF-8 BOM and normalize Unicode to NFC
    text = unicodedata.normalize('NFC', content.lstrip('\ufeff'))
    # 2. Exclude volatile frontmatter fields
    text = strip_volatile_frontmatter(text)
    # 3. Normalize line endings to LF
    text = text.replace("\r\n", "\n").replace("\r", "\n")
    # 4. Clean line trailing whitespace preserving Markdown double-space breaks
    lines = []
    for line in text.split("\n"):
        if line.endswith("  ") and not line.endswith("   "):
            lines.append(line.rstrip() + "  ")
        else:
            lines.append(line.rstrip())
    normalized = "\n".join(lines).strip()
    return hashlib.sha256(normalized.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
```
Editing or regenerating a plan alters its canonical hash, invalidating the approval automatically.

### 4.4 Remedial Foundation Breakouts vs. Graduation for Embedded Sections
In alignment with ADR-011 §3.5:
- If `approve-pmp` rejects an integrated PMP due to a substandard embedded `pmp_section`, the failure is attributed to that specific section. That failure triggers a **remedial foundation breakout** to gather missing foundation data and **regenerate that section** within `.staging/pmp-sections/` for re-synthesis into the PMP. Regenerating a section triggers `STALE_DEPENDENCY` on any downstream plans.
- Only if the section **outgrows its cap** (or if human leadership explicitly decides the domain complexity warrants standalone governance) does it **graduate** to a `full_plan` route. Upon graduation, the section moves from staging to `docs/project-docs/management-plans/<Name>.md`, requiring an individual `approve-subsidiary-plan` gate; any downstream approvals that recorded its staged hash are flagged as `STALE_DEPENDENCY` and re-verified against the new standalone plan.

### 4.5 Transitive Dependency Invalidation & Scope Bounding
Because plans form a causal dependency DAG (Scope $\to$ Schedule $\to$ Cost $\to$ Support plans):
- Each approval `Episode` records the canonical content hash of its immediate upstream nodes, whether a standalone `full_plan` or a staged `pmp_section`.
- If an immediate upstream node is tailored as `not_applicable`, it contributes the hash of its approved omission rationale episode, or the DAG edge bypasses it to the nearest active ancestor.
- **Transitive Status Propagation:** `STALE_DEPENDENCY` propagates transitively down the DAG as a status: if Scope changes, Schedule becomes stale; because Schedule is stale, Cost becomes transitively stale upon verification check, even before Schedule is regenerated.
- **Bounded Invalidation Scope:** Dependency invalidation follows the approved PMBOK causal DAG and **does not propagate beyond impacted downstream branches**. Changes to an operational or support plan (e.g. Communications 10.1 or Quality 8.1) do not invalidate core baselines (Scope, Schedule, Cost), nor do orthogonal plans invalidate each other.
- **Asynchronous Staging & Ordering:** A downstream `full_plan` (e.g. Cost) may be approved while an upstream `pmp_section` (e.g. Scope, Schedule) is still an unapproved staged draft. If `approve-pmp` later rejects that upstream section and causes it to be regenerated, downstream approvals automatically become stale and require re-verification.

### 4.6 Plan Governance State Lifecycle
To eliminate ambiguity across drafting, review, dependency cascades, and adaptation, each subsidiary plan progresses through an explicit finite state machine:
- `DRAFTED`: Plan file generated on disk or staged in `.staging/pmp-sections/`, awaiting evaluation.
- `UNDER_REVIEW`: Candidate `DecisionFrame` constructed and presented to `approve-subsidiary-plan`.
- `APPROVED`: Hash-verified `approve_plan` episode recorded with zero active staleness flags.
- `REVISION_REQUESTED`: Review outcome requiring localized edits before re-submitting to `UNDER_REVIEW`.
- `REJECTED`: Terminal review outcome requiring fundamental structural redesign or tailoring re-routing.
- `STALE_DEPENDENCY`: Upstream causal ancestor in the DAG was regenerated or edited; approval validity is suspended until re-verification.
- `REAPPROVAL_REQUIRED`: Plan content was edited or regenerated; new canonical content hash requires evaluation.

### 4.7 Adaptation re-approves only what it regenerates
In `--mode adapt`, only the plans checked in `Adaptation-Plan.md` are regenerated. Each regenerated plan loses its hash approval and must be re-approved; unchanged plans with valid upstream dependencies keep theirs.

### 4.8 Legacy Bootstrap Baseline Retained for Historical Traceability
`PMP-BL-v1.0.0` was synthesized over stub-generated plans without plan-level approvals, and it was never approved through `approve-pmp`. This ADR classifies `PMP-BL-v1.0.0` as a **legacy bootstrap baseline retained for historical traceability that carries no approved governance status**, to be formally superseded by the first baseline formalized under this sequence and approved through a built `approve-pmp`.

### 4.9 Unit of Attribution: Plan-Level vs. Integration-Level Defects
To ensure accurate credit assignment without misattributing emergent flaws:
- **Plan-Level Defects:** Defects localized to an individual subsidiary plan or staged section (e.g., floor violations, missing WBS elements, unexpanded placeholders, incomplete stakeholder registers). Attributed directly to `approve-subsidiary-plan` for that plan or to a remedial foundation breakout for that section (§4.4).
- **Integration-Level Defects:** Emergent cross-cutting defects that arise only at the synthesis boundary (e.g., conflicting schedule milestones vs. cost expenditure baselines, mutually contradictory resource assumptions, duplicate RACI responsibilities across plans). Attributed directly to `approve-pmp` as an integration defect, without artificially forcing blame onto an otherwise compliant individual plan.
- **Per-Plan Revision Comparison:** Successive `DecisionResult` files for a revised plan are compared using ADR-005 `compare_decision_results()`, tracking measured progression or regression per plan with bounded retries and human escalation.
- **Evidence Boundary:** These are `full`-authority decisions; under ADR-001 §4.2 they are scored audit evidence and do not enter the REINFORCE learner.

### 4.10 Decoupled Architectural Boundary (Inversion of Control)
To preserve architectural purity and prevent domain-to-storage coupling, the PMBOK domain package (`pmbok/`) must not import `agent_learning.store.LearningStore`. The orchestration CLI (`scripts/orchestrate_pmbok.py`) acts as the composition root: it queries `LearningStore` for approval episodes, verifies canonical content hashes, and passes validated plan states into PMBOK synthesis functions as pure domain data arguments.

## 5. Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Reason for Rejection |
|---|---|
| **Nine separate policies, one per plan** | Duplicates action taxonomy nine times, multiplies policy lineage overhead, and conflicts with ADR-001 §4.1. |
| **Monolithic 9-plan gate (ADR-013 draft v1)** | Directly contradicts ADR-011 tailoring; halts orchestration for any project with `pmp_section` or `not_applicable` routes. |
| **Keep the status quo: judge all plans implicitly through `approve-pmp`** | The defect this ADR resolves. A single integrated judgment lets stub content and failed generations slip into baselines undetected. |
| **Let an LLM agent approve plans with no human adjudication** | Violates ADR-008 Invariant #4 (human leadership retains approval authority for planning baselines). |
| **Naive Raw Byte Hashing** | Fails across platforms when Git checks out CRLF on Windows and LF on Linux, invalidating legitimate approvals without text changes. |

## 6. Consequences

### Positive
- Rejection or revision requests point directly at the offending plan and criterion, separating plan-level from integration-level defects (§4.9).
- Fully harmonized with ADR-011 tailored routes (`full_plan`, `pmp_section`, `not_applicable`).
- Canonical hashing prevents false-positive hash invalidations across operating systems.
- Stub or failed plans cannot reach an approved PMP baseline.
- Batch review protocol protects human attention while preserving granular per-plan episode attribution.

### Negative
- Increases procedural steps for projects requiring multiple `full_plan` artifacts.
- The orchestrator interface in ADR-008 §4.2, BRU-008-04, and Story 8.1 require amendment to reflect route-aware multi-step orchestration.
- Imposes a functional requirement on `approve-pmp` (designed, not yet built) to attribute integrated PMP rejections to specific offending sections or integration-level synthesis conflicts.

## 7. Architectural Invariants

1. **Route-Aware Formalization Precondition:** The PMP is not synthesized or formalized unless every subsidiary plan designated `full_plan` holds a current, hash-verified, non-stale approval episode (no active `STALE_DEPENDENCY`), every plan designated `pmp_section` is present in staging (`.staging/pmp-sections/`) and non-stub (with any floor/cap deviations surfaced as flags for human review at `approve-pmp`), and every plan designated `not_applicable` has an approved tailoring rationale (ADR-011).
2. **Permanent Human Authority:** `approve-subsidiary-plan` is `full` authority and permanently `requires_human_approval: true`; no autonomous path approves a plan (ADR-001, ADR-008 Invariant #4).
3. **Storage Law (ADR-002 Invariant #2):** Approval evidence is stored strictly as `Episode` records; zero new record types, tables, or database containers.
4. **Canonical Hash Invariance:** Approval validity is bound to the canonical content hash (LF-normalized, NFC Unicode, UTF-8 BOM stripped, whitespace-stripped preserving Markdown double-space breaks, volatile frontmatter excluded); raw byte hashing is prohibited.
5. **Hard Constraint on Technical Deficiencies vs. Floor Flags:** A stub-generated, bracketed-placeholder-containing (`Stub content for Artifact`, `[Insert here]`, `[TBD]`, `[TODO]`), or generation-failed plan cannot be approved and blocks formalization. In alignment with ADR-011 §3.2, word count deviations below floors or above caps, as well as provisional unbracketed tokens, are flags with evidence, not hard blocks: they do not mechanically rule out approval, but require a recorded human rationale if approved.
6. **Remedial Foundation Breakouts vs. Graduation:** An embedded `pmp_section` rejected during `approve-pmp` triggers a remedial foundation breakout to regenerate that section within the PMP. Only sections that outgrow their cap (or are explicitly designated by human decision) graduate to a standalone `full_plan` route (ADR-011 §3.5).
7. **Transitive Dependency Integrity & Scope Bounding:** Regeneration of an upstream dependency (whether `full_plan` or staged `pmp_section`) invalidates downstream approvals via transitive `STALE_DEPENDENCY` propagation strictly along impacted causal DAG branches, requiring re-verification before formalization.
8. **Decoupled Module Purity:** The PMBOK domain package (`pmbok/`) must not import `LearningStore`; approval episode checks are mediated via an inversion-of-control protocol in the CLI layer.
9. **Plan Lifecycle State Determinism:** Subsidiary plans must transition strictly through the explicit governance lifecycle states (`DRAFTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `STALE_DEPENDENCY`, `REAPPROVAL_REQUIRED`); formalization is blocked if any required plan is in an unapproved or stale state.
